## Writing Structure Framework

### Standard Research Report Structure
1. **Executive Summary** (conclusions first, 1 page max)
2. **Introduction** (why this matters, scope)
3. **Methodology** (how research was conducted)
4. **Findings** (what was discovered, by section)
5. **Limitations** (what wasn't covered, caveats)
6. **Conclusion** (so what, next steps)
7. **Appendix** (detailed data, methodology)

### Section Writing Order
1. Read verified claims and citations
2. Identify key messages per section
3. Draft conclusions first (they anchor everything)
4. Write supporting evidence
5. Add methodology and limitations
6. Review for flow

## Citation Standards

| Verification Status | Citation Format |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| VERIFIED | [Source Name, Year] with link |
| UNVERIFIED | [Source Name, Year] — unverified |
| DISPUTED | [Source Name, Year] — disputed, see limitations |
| FALSE | Do not cite; flag for removal |

## Accessibility Checklist

- [ ] All jargon defined on first use
- [ ] Acronyms spelled out on first use
- [ ] Statistical findings explained in plain terms
- [ ] Visualizations described in text
- [ ] Reading level appropriate for audience

## Tone Guide

| Audience | Tone | Complexity |
|----------|------|------------|
| Executive | Conclusion-first, minimal jargon | Low |
| Technical | Full methodology, precise terms | High |
| Mixed | Layered (summary + detail) | Medium |
| General Public | Analogies, no jargon | Minimal |

## Common Pitfalls

- **Abstract to concrete:** Don't stay at 30,000 feet
- **Passive voice:** "was discovered" vs "we found"
- **Weasel words:** "evidence suggests" when you mean "data shows"
- **Overclaiming:** "revolutionary" when you mean "notable"

## Guardrail Reminder

Do not write a claim that exceeds its evidence level. If fact-check says "unverified," you write "unverified" — not "likely."
